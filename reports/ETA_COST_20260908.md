## Why late-epoch averaging does not eliminate the work

The paper specifies96 evaluations per simulation checkpoint and three training
seeds for final models, averaging over ten late epochs. These are30 distinct
checkpoint evaluations, not30 repetitions of the released checkpoint and not
96episodes per GPU. Arithmetic aggregation of saved scores is CPU work and cheap.

For just native plus one intervention on Reach and Reach-Wall:

`96 × 3 seeds × 10 checkpoints × 2 tasks × 2 conditions = 11,520 episodes`.

At302seconds per episode, that is**966.4GPU-hours**. If all17GPUs could sustain
that measured throughput on this work alone with perfect allocation, it would
take**56.85hours**; maintaining the current account rate would cost about**$395.80**.
This is a conditional workload projection, not an optimized lower bound, a
measured full-history speed, a full-study ETA or permission to spend that amount.
It excludes training, refitting, additional controls/tasks and confirmation.
Different checkpoints may have different timing; model/hardware changes are not
silently assumed equivalent. The full six-task end-to-end ETA remains unmeasured.

The narrower final-checkpoint three-seed report would have one-tenth as many
evaluation episodes for the same two-task/two-condition scope, but it is a
different report from the ten-epoch aggregate. The authors report that narrower
comparison separately inTable12. The user's required full-history objective has
not been replaced by that cheaper scope.
