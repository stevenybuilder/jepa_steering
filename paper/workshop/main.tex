\begin{abstract}
How does an activation edit inside a frozen world model reach its planner's decisions? We study this computation in JEPA-WM, separating internal reconstruction, future prediction, goal scoring, and closed-loop behavior. Earlier layer sweeps show a task-dependent early-to-late gradient in forecast correction. A 64-context component-replay experiment finds that candidate-common rank-four corrections reproduce almost all measured candidate-centered goal-cost changes: reconstruction scores are 0.983 on Reach and 0.998 on Reach-Wall. Separately, archived costs certify an unchanged winning candidate in 184 of 192 fixed banks. Global ranking stability need not preserve the elite set: controlled donor-condition substitutions retain high rank agreement while changing top-ten membership. A second 64-context experiment holds weights and action perturbations fixed: FP32 favors cubic activation reconstruction at every block, BF16 reverses the comparison, and rounding only the FP32 output does not reproduce the full effect. A separate protected panel of 3,072 arm evaluations on 384 fresh scenarios changes which scenarios succeed without establishing a net success gain. These measurements locate distinct prediction-to-decision sensitivities; they do not establish a universal physical coordinate system or explain the aggregate protected outcome.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Latent world models select actions by predicting the consequences of candidate plans. In a JEPA world model, an encoder and an action-conditioned dynamics predictor produce a latent future; a planner compares that future with a goal \citep{zhou2024dinowm,terver2025jepawm}. This offers an intervention target: improve the imagined future by editing an internal representation while leaving model weights frozen. The relevant question, however, is not only whether prediction improves. It is whether the changed prediction changes the \emph{relative costs} of candidate actions in a useful way.

Activation steering already changes frozen-policy behavior. COAST steers a policy action expert using success/failure-derived conceptors \citep{miao2026coast}. Our edit instead enters a predictor scored by a separate planner. Both are latent steering; the distinction is the computation between the edited state and the executed action. Physical-variable probes in video encoders \citep{joseph2026interpretingphysics} and curved activation interpolation \citep{wurgaft2026manifoldsteering} also motivate geometric questions, but their endpoints do not establish better robotic planning.

Our findings are: \textbf{(1)} forecast correction has a task-dependent layer and rank structure; \textbf{(2)} candidate-common corrections change relative goal costs, and adaptive search can diverge despite initial selection stability; and \textbf{(3)} local reconstruction and downstream forecast utility behave differently across numerical conditions. We place these measurements alongside separately frozen behavioral confirmation. We neither pool development and confirmation nor infer a causal chain across experiments that changed operators, inputs, or planner randomness.

\section{Model, interventions, and evidence populations}
\label{sec:design}

\paragraph{Frozen predictor and planner.}
We use released JEPA-WM checkpoints and task-specific fitted interventions. Reach and Reach-Wall share a checkpoint, not their fitted operators. DINO visual features and proprioceptive features feed a six-block action-conditioned predictor. All base encoders and predictor weights remain frozen during intervention; this does not imply that every encoder was frozen during original training.

The analyzed MetaWorld planner uses the actual weighted goal cost
\begin{equation}
 C(a_{1:H})=\MSE(\hat z^v_H,z^v_g)+0.1\,\MSE(\hat z^p_H,z^p_g),
 \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
with each MSE averaged over its modality's dimensions. The cost is \emph{not} visual-only. Recorded-future proprioceptive error and goal-directed planning cost nevertheless use different targets, weights, and action inputs. CEM uses 300 candidates. MetaWorld uses 15 optimization iterations and seven planning calls over the recorded 100-elementary-step episodes; navigation uses 30 iterations. Imagined steps, simulator steps, CEM iterations, and replanning calls are distinct units.

\paragraph{Refined rank-four correction.}
The later fixed-response operator edits the B3 output at H3. With native field \(h\), orthonormal four-direction basis \(B\), and \(\phi(h)\) consisting of an intercept and three standardized native-feature scores, an offline map \(W\) gives
\begin{equation}
 d(h)=BW\phi(h),\qquad h'=h+\eta\,d(h)/\|d(h)\|_2,
 \label{eq:refined}
\end{equation}
using the frozen zero-edit rule below the correction threshold. The edit covers all 256 visual patches within the existing batch, without online response probes or native-shadow forecasts. Its random-subspace comparator has separate response calibration and matched support, rank, basis-projector spectrum, and prescribed dose---not identical coefficient-map singular values. This successor is not an equivalent implementation of the earlier online response solver.

\paragraph{Visual/action interventions.}
A fit-only maximum-covariance direction pair edits the H3 visual predictor input and B3 action-conditioning vector. Unscaled joint, visual-only, and action-only arms form a fixed-dose factorial. Equal-budget coupling scales both standardized doses by \(1/\sqrt{2}\); its random-direction comparator uses the same sites and dose policy. This comparison is distinct from the unscaled factorial (Table~\ref{fig:arms}).

\paragraph{Separate populations and units.}
Fit trajectories supply directions, readouts, and calibration. Separate recorded-action development lineages measure forecast effects and inform choices; fitting-disjoint does not mean selection-protected. Corrected primary offline pools contain 33 Reach, 27 Reach-Wall, and 21 Push-T lineages. Geometry analyses also include 192 Wall and 200 PointMaze lineages. The archived decision diagnostic has 96 canonical development scenarios per MetaWorld task, each with one shared initial bank of 300 action sequences. Protected confirmation instead uses 96 \emph{new} scientific scenarios per task on Reach, Reach-Wall, PointMaze, and Wall. The earlier six-task behavioral table and interrupted native-only cohort remain separate.

The archival CPU analyses recount hash-verified records. New GPU diagnostics separately reexecute the frozen predictor on 64 canonical development contexts (IDs 0--31 per MetaWorld task), with an eight-context actual-CEM pilot (IDs 0--3 per task). Controlled arithmetic uses all 64 contexts; action-conditioning specificity uses 16 (IDs 0--7 per task), each with original and freshly seeded candidate banks. Fresh candidates are not fresh scenarios. These diagnostics do not execute physical actions. Offline effects use source lineages as units; candidate costs and behavior use scenarios. Candidates, heads, horizons, and calls are not independent replicates. Follow-up protocols specify their uncertainty families before outcomes; the studies remain development analyses, not replacements for protected confirmation.

\section{Finding 1: forecast correction has layer and rank structure}
\label{sec:layers}

A recount verifies all 576 task/precision/support/modality/horizon cells in the earlier layer grid. In its singleton \emph{rank-one} sweep, H6 proprioceptive-error reduction decreases monotonically from B0 to B5 on both MetaWorld tasks in both numerical conditions (Figure~\ref{fig:layers}). Reach B0/B5 reductions are 3.026\%/0.475\% in BF16 and 3.050\%/0.494\% in FP32; Reach-Wall reductions are 1.526\%/0.298\% and 1.334\%/0.228\%. Original BF16 simultaneous B0-minus-B5 intervals are \([1.769,3.333]\) and \([0.243,2.214]\) percentage points of native MSE.

This is intervention susceptibility, not a uniquely semantic layer. Layer-specific fits, representation scale, dose realization, and downstream computation all enter the comparison. Reach B0 versus B1 remains unresolved, and Push-T does not reproduce the MetaWorld H6 benefit. Endpoints before H3 precede the intervention.

The later \emph{rank-four fixed-response} successor is evaluated at B3, not across this layer sweep. It reduces development H6 proprioceptive MSE by 2.360\% on Reach and 2.192\% on Reach-Wall while removing online response probes. Neither result establishes that moving the successor to B0 would improve planning.

\section{Finding 2: initial stability does not determine adaptive search}
\label{sec:decision}

\paragraph{An exact sufficient certificate.}
For a fixed candidate bank, let \(C_i\) be native cost, \(i^\star\) its unique winner, and \(\delta_i=C'_i-C_i\) the edit-induced change. Define
\begin{equation}
 m=\min_{j\ne i^\star}(C_j-C_{i^\star}),\qquad
 r=\max_i\delta_i-\min_i\delta_i.
 \label{eq:margin}
\end{equation}
If \(r<m\), the winner cannot change, because every competitor satisfies
\begin{equation}
 C'_j-C'_{i^\star}
 =(C_j-C_{i^\star})+(\delta_j-\delta_{i^\star})
 \ge m-r>0.
 \label{eq:certificate}
\end{equation}
This sufficient inequality concerns the saved finite bank, not generalization. Failure to certify does not imply a flip; actual selection and tie handling are checked separately.
The diagnostic selects the bank's best candidate. CEM instead updates its proposal from ten elites, so preserving the best index alone does not certify an unchanged CEM update or final plan.

\paragraph{Measured cost changes are small relative to ranking.}
We analyze all 192 completed development scenarios and four non-native diagnostic arms (768 paired comparisons). For refined editing, \(r<m\) in 94/96 Reach and 90/96 Reach-Wall banks; winners actually change in 0/96 and 1/96. The mean scenario-level ratio
\[
 100\,\RMS_i(\delta_i-\bar\delta)/\sqrt{\Var_i(C_i)}
\]
is 0.2466\% and 0.2786\%. Coupling certifies 82/96 and 81/96 banks and changes 1/96 and 2/96 winners. Table~\ref{tab:margin} retains both random controls.

\paragraph{Shared coefficients need not produce shared costs.}
A separate decomposition of 768 protected arm records finds that 98.855\% and 99.615\% of refined coefficient energy on Reach and Reach-Wall is shared across candidates within a call. This is coefficient geometry, not the fraction of decision-irrelevant information. Even a constant final-latent translation \(d\) changes squared goal cost by
\begin{equation}
 \|z_i+d-g\|^2-\|z_i-g\|^2
 =2d^\top(z_i-g)+\|d\|^2,
 \label{eq:translation}
\end{equation}
whose first term varies across candidates. The actual edit also passes through a nonlinear predictor. Margin measurements therefore add information that coefficient energy alone cannot supply.

Archived decision banks and protected episodes differ in inputs and planner RNG. Protected records did not retain numeric candidate costs or elite identities. Thus the certificate cannot explain which protected scenarios were rescued or broken. A low first-bank flip count also does not establish replanning drift as their cause.

\subsection{Component replay and actual adaptive search}
\label{sec:replay}

\paragraph{A shared field carries relative-score changes.}
We now test the common-shift question directly on all 64 fixed development contexts. For each learned or calibrated-random correction, cache its native H3/B3 field and replay the full field, zero field, candidate-common mean, and candidate-centered remainder on the same 300 actions. Cached-full and zero controls reproduce their reference forecasts and scores exactly. Neither component is renormalized, and no operator is refitted.

Let $u$ be the full intervention's cost change, centered across candidates, and $u_k$ the corresponding change for component $k$. We report $R_k=1-\|u-u_k\|_2^2/\|u\|_2^2$, retaining undefined zero-denominator cases and allowing negative values. This is a reconstruction score, not a probability. For learned edits, common-only $R$ is 0.9827 on Reach (marginal 95\% interval $[0.9793,0.9858]$) and 0.9975 on Reach-Wall ($[0.9971,0.9980]$); centered-only values are 0.0218 and 0.0135. Common-field energy fractions are 99.53\% and 99.78\%. Random-subspace common-only scores are similarly high, 0.9795 and 0.9854. These results establish the common component's effect at its natural energy, not superiority over equal-energy centered steering.

\paragraph{Small initial changes can accompany divergent adaptive searches.}
After an initial eight-case pilot, a separately frozen extension reruns all three arms on the same receiving GPU for 56 new development contexts ($n=28$ per task). Each search uses fifteen CEM iterations, 300 candidates and ten elites; traced/untraced selected-plan and RNG parity pass for all 336 searches. The original eight cases remain separate from primary inference. Every arm starts from identical candidate actions, but initial elite sets need not be identical: mean learned/random overlap with the native top ten is 9.893/9.857 on Reach and 9.964/9.929 on Reach-Wall.

Later proposals adapt to each arm's scores, so later candidate IDs are not matched actions. Figure~\ref{fig:cem-expansion} retains all fifteen iterations. Returned three-step, 60-coordinate prefixes have mean RMS differences from unsteered of 0.697/0.578 for learned/random edits on Reach and 0.500/0.217 on Reach-Wall. These normalized model-action differences are not physical displacements or success gains. All six prespecified learned-minus-random intervals include zero (Table~\ref{tab:cem-extension}); the positive marginal Reach-Wall prefix contrast does not survive the registered family correction. Search-path changes therefore extend beyond the original eight cases, without establishing greater divergence or a consistent entropy effect for learned edits over calibrated random edits.

\subsection{Action conditions affect rankings and elite membership differently}

In a separate 16-context experiment, each candidate receives the next candidate's newest action-derived AdaLN condition at H3, one block at a time. A private-generator isotropic perturbation matches each donor-minus-recipient L2 norm. All six blocks and both original and freshly seeded 300-action banks are retained. This is donor interchange within a context, not an intervention on explicit action tokens or semantic physical labels.

At B1 and B4, donor substitution disrupts native official-cost rankings more than the random edit: excess Spearman loss is 0.0071--0.0084 across the two tasks and two banks. Six-layer simultaneous intervals exclude zero in these cells, but no layer reaches the prespecified 0.05 practical flag. Native rank agreement remains at least 0.98979 in every official-cost cell. At B1, however, donor edits lose a mean 1.0--1.125 additional members of the native top ten relative to random edits. Small global rank changes and a changed elite set can coexist; neither establishes physical action quality.

Delivered-norm mismatch is not supported here: the maximum relative error is $2.72\times10^{-8}$. Distributional mismatch remains. The affine action encoder maps 20 input coordinates into 400 condition coordinates; isotropic noise need not lie in its at-most-20-dimensional reachable subspace. Matching total L2 therefore does not match action-reachable dose. Complete specificity curves and uncertainty appear in Appendix~\ref{app:condition}; the following separately frozen study adds range controls.

\subsection{Coherent action changes require consistent history}
\label{sec:history}

An H3 action appears twice in the predictor's two-frame context: newest at H3, then older at H4. On the same 16 contexts and both fixed banks, we compare coherent raw H3 action substitution with donor-condition replacement at H3 only or at both appearances. All-block persistent replacement reproduces the raw-action counterfactual byte-exactly through H6. All-block H3-only replacement matches at H3 but has H6 reconstruction $R=0.5029$--$0.5079$ across task/bank cells (Figure~\ref{fig:history}). Here $R=1-\MSE(\hat z_{\rm patch},\hat z_{\rm cf})/\MSE(\hat z_{\rm native},\hat z_{\rm cf})$, using ratios of candidate means under the official modality weighting. This is a predictor-internal counterfactual, not its physical correctness.

The six-layer contrasts retain a 72-cell simultaneous primary family (two tasks, two banks, six layers, three contrasts). Input-range membership is not a global activation manifold, and off-range does not mean causally inert. Together, these interventions identify an action-history dependency within this predictor. They neither reproduce the older static steering operators nor establish the cause of protected outcome changes.

\section{Finding 3: reconstruction and forecast utility separate}
\label{sec:geometry}

\paragraph{A controlled arithmetic comparison.}
The new 64-context experiment fixes model weights, encoded inputs, the literal first action coordinate, four symmetric perturbations ($\pm0.1,\pm0.05$), and reconstruction coefficients. At every block, it compares strict FP32, actual BF16-autocast computation, and FP32 fields rounded to BF16 then restored to FP32. Each condition reconstructs its own unperturbed center; no fitted bank or dose-normalized correction changes between conditions.

FP32 favors cubic reconstruction at all six blocks: mean log cubic/linear MSE ratios span $-5.40$ to $-7.89$. Output round-tripping moves these ratios near equality ($-0.098$ to $+0.010$), whereas actual BF16 produces $+0.160$ to $+0.306$. The actual-BF16-minus-rounded contrast is $+0.224$ to $+0.336$ across all twelve task/block cells, with six-layer-corrected lower bounds above the prespecified 0.1 log-unit margin. Thus output rounding degrades the apparent reconstruction advantage but does not quantitatively reproduce the actual BF16 result. The experiment isolates numerical-condition sensitivity, not an individual kernel or a physical manifold.

\paragraph{Precision-sensitive local interpolation.}
The action-response experiment withholds the center of a four-donor activation path. Coordinates \(-1,-\tfrac12,\tfrac12,1\) index frozen local action offsets. Equal-anchor linear reconstruction averages the donors. The archived cubic estimator at zero is
\begin{equation}
 \hat h_{\mathrm{center}}=-\tfrac16h_{-1}
 +\tfrac23h_{-1/2}+\tfrac23h_{1/2}-\tfrac16h_1.
 \label{eq:center}
\end{equation}
At this symmetric evaluation point the same functional is obtained by quadratic least squares. It does not uniquely identify third-order dynamics or a dense activation manifold.

Across all five tasks, raw omitted-activation MSE is 35.15--46.77\% \emph{higher} than linear reconstruction in BF16, but 93.96--99.99\% \emph{lower} in FP32. This does not correspond to a similar improvement in future prediction. Under matched requested doses, the H6 proprioceptive-MSE advantage over the linear arm is small and mixed: BF16 task means range from \(-0.0213\%\) to \(+0.0264\%\) of native future MSE; FP32 ranges from \(-0.0141\%\) to \(+0.0051\%\). The two endpoints have different targets and denominators (Appendix~\ref{app:geometry}), so these scales are not an attenuation coefficient.

\paragraph{Requested dose is not exact realized-energy equivalence.}
The precision conditions also differ in fitted banks and prescribed dose, preventing attribution solely to rounding. In FP32 Reach/Reach-Wall, the cubic arm's per-window realized-energy tolerance passes only 49.0\%/50.7\% of windows, versus 98.1\%/98.4\% for linear reconstruction, despite small mean realized/requested L2 discrepancies. The comparison is therefore labelled by its actual requested-dose contract, not perfect per-window energy matching.

\paragraph{Output nonadditivity differs from loss interaction.}
For the unscaled visual/action factorial, let \(e=z_0-y\), \(v=z_V-z_0\), \(a=z_A-z_0\), and \(q=z_{VA}-z_V-z_A+z_0\). Squared-error interaction obeys
\begin{equation}
 L(VA)-L(V)-L(A)+L(0)
 =2v^\top a+2(e+v+a)^\top q+\|q\|^2,
 \label{eq:factorial}
\end{equation}
with the endpoint's dimensional averaging. Thus loss interaction can be nonzero even when output interaction \(q\) vanishes.

Saved H6 output-interaction squared norms, divided by native proprioceptive MSE, are 0.419\%, 0.512\%, and 5.020\% in BF16 for Reach, Reach-Wall, and Push-T. Corresponding FP32 values are \(4.82\times10^{-6}\%\), \(1.07\times10^{-5}\%\), and \(0.00256\%\). These establish precision-conditioned differences in retained scalar diagnostics, not that rounding caused them. Missing horizons remain missing; original tensors are not reconstructed from scalars. Together, these results distinguish local interpolation quality, the delivered activation intervention, and its downstream forecast utility.
The loss terms can nearly cancel: BF16 Push-T has a \(+2.460\%\) additive cross-term and \(-2.509\%\) nonadditive remainder, yielding only \(-0.049\%\) net H6 loss interaction. Figure~\ref{fig:factorial} retains all seven source conditions, not just this illustrative case.

\section{Protected behavior is an independent endpoint}
\label{sec:behavior}

The complete protected panel contains 384 fresh scenarios across Reach, Reach-Wall, PointMaze, and Wall and eight arms per scenario: 3,072 scientific evaluations. Input, operator, checkpoint, source, RNG, and analysis identities were frozen before scientific outcomes. All arms of each scenario use the same physical GPU, strict FP32, and TF32 disabled. Excluded engineering inputs validate native repeats, zero-dose identity, hook/reference agreement, and dose before science.

The frozen family contains 48 paired contrasts: seven non-native arms versus native, two learned-versus-random comparisons, and three unscaled factorial contrasts per task. All 48 Bonferroni-simultaneous 95\% percentile intervals, from 20,000 paired-scenario bootstrap draws, include zero. The panel therefore does not establish a reliable gain under its registered analysis; neither does it establish equivalence or an exactly zero effect. One released checkpoint per task and 96 paired scenarios do not provide independent-training-seed replication or precise detection of small effects.

The edits nonetheless change which scenarios succeed. On fresh Reach, native and refined both succeed in 52/96 scenarios, with 21 native failures rescued and 21 native successes lost. Fresh native rates are 54.17\%, 29.17\%, 86.46\%, and 81.25\% in task order. The old six-task native row is a different measurement. Both cohort and planner RNG changed; baseline differences cannot be attributed solely to overfitting, hardware, or new initial states.

\section{Implications and limits}

An internal edit should be measured at the intervention site, at its downstream forecast target, and at the planner's relative scoring geometry. A real cost change can be too small to cross a particular selection boundary. Conversely, low coefficient variation across candidates does not make a shared edit irrelevant to ranking. These are testable distinctions, not evidence for a universal physical coordinate system.

\paragraph{Relation to latent steering.}
COAST uses outcome-labelled conceptors in a policy action expert \citep{miao2026coast}; our recorded-forecast fit precedes a separate sampling planner. This motivates an objective-mismatch hypothesis but does not explain the different reported gains. The conceptor is also a full matrix: low effective spectral rank does not make its displacement identical to a rank-four edit. Physical-variable steering in video representations evaluates probe readouts and plausibility, not robot success \citep{joseph2026interpretingphysics}. Manifold steering's curved-versus-straight interpolation and vision readout likewise differ from closed-loop control \citep{wurgaft2026manifoldsteering}.

\paragraph{Prospective questions.}
Model-native coordinates may expose useful variables, as suitable reference frames do for Othello representations \citep{nanda2023emergentlinear}. Establishing them here requires held-out physical labels and a predicted causal consequence, not semantic names for PCA axes. Our completed fixed-bank replay isolates shared and centered edits, and the small CEM follow-up measures adaptive-plan divergence. Simulator forks would additionally test physical candidate ranking rather than latent ranking alone; that remains prospective.

Adaptation is distinct again. AdaJEPA uses observed transitions to update a world model and replan \citep{wang2026adajepa}. Test-time-training layers and fast weights change state or memory machinery \citep{sun2024tttlayers,ba2016fastweights}, not merely fixed steering coefficients. Fair comparisons must match observation access and optimization/latency budgets, and separate real observed history from candidate-specific imagined histories.

A smaller end-to-end latent model such as LeWorldModel could reduce the cost of a future controlled mechanism study \citep{maes2026lewm}. Memory Maze would be an optional, distinct testbed only if partial observability and long-term memory become the target variables \citep{pasukonis2022memorymaze}. Neither is a tested extension here, and current PointMaze results are not Memory Maze evidence.

\paragraph{Scope.}
Early rank-one sweeps, later rank-four fitting, archival candidate banks, and protected rollouts have complementary but noninterchangeable scopes. Controlled replay and arithmetic interventions identify effects within their fixed inputs; they do not identify a universal physics layer or the cause of protected outcome changes. Their contribution is to make intermediate relationships measurable: where a predictor can be changed, whether local reconstruction improves a future target, and whether its costs cross a decision boundary.

\paragraph{Execution and scaling.}
The frozen PyTorch inference workloads used independent whole-scenario GPU replicas, with 300 candidates batched inside each forward call. All eight arms of a scenario stayed on the same physical device. Pinned cloud staging and asynchronous hash-verified preservation overlapped independent execution; they did not alter scientific inputs. Strict FP32 and TF32-off settings were retained. We applied the workload-first scaling principles of the Ultra-Scale Playbook and \emph{How To Scale Your Model} \citep{huggingfaceultrascale,austin2025gpus}: distinguish memory capacity, compute, communication, and serial orchestration, then parallelize independent work. Training-oriented gradient sharding and model parallelism were unnecessary because the model fits per device and evaluation has no backward pass. No JAX rewrite, FlashAttention replacement, or measured kernel-level speedup is claimed. Any future compiler, precision, or parallelism change must pass receiving and replay parity checks before scientific comparison.

The code and aggregate package is maintained at \url{https://github.com/stevenybuilder/jepa_steering}, currently a private repository. This does not claim that all raw archives are public. New replay or adaptation work requires separately specified inputs, parity checks, and an evidence label. Unfinished experiments are not counted as results here.
